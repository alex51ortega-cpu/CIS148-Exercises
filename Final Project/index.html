Mt. Everest

Mount Everest is famous for being the tallest mountain in the world, and it is a pretty amazing place to study. It sits right on the border between Nepal and China in a mountain range called the Himalayas. The official height of the peak is 8,848.86 meters, which is so high up that it actually touches the jet stream where the winds are crazy strong. Because it is so extreme, the weather up there is super dangerous and freezing cold.

People from all over the world travel to Everest because they want to challenge themselves and see if they can make it to the top. But the mountain is more than just a giant pile of rock and ice; it also has a lot of history and cultural meaning. The local people who live near it consider the mountain sacred. In Nepal, they call it *Sagarmatha*, which means "Goddess of the Sky," and in Tibet, its name is *Chomolungma*, which translates to "Goddess Mother of the World."

Quick Facts:

  • Growing in height: Believe it or not, tectonic plates are still moving under the mountain, which makes Everest grow about 4 millimeters taller every single year!
  • Forgotten equipment: Over decades of expeditions, hundreds of discarded tents, broken ladders, and old camping gear have been left behind on the high slopes, sparking major cleanup efforts today.
  • Oxygen tanks: Because the air is so thin near the summit, most climbers have to carry heavy, pressurized neon-orange oxygen tanks just to keep their brains and muscles working.
  • First person on top: In 1953, Sir Edmund Hillary from New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay Sherpa from Nepal became the first verified human beings to ever successfully stand on the summit.
